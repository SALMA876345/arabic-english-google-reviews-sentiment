# Known Limitations

These are structural constraints identified during dataset/literature audit, not yet-to-be-fixed bugs. They must be carried into the eventual paper's Limitations section rather than silently resolved or hidden.

1. **No large-scale, human-annotated, document-level Arabic review dataset exists in this domain.** The best candidate (Zenodo 17011574) is high-quality but small (3,543 reviews) and aspect-level, not document-level by design. The only sizeable alternative (Zenodo 22061934, 8,671 reviews) is majority LLM-labeled. This is a genuine data-scarcity problem in the field, not an oversight of this search.

2. **The primary English dataset (UCSD/McAuley Google Local) has no confirmed license.** Direct inspection of its repository and documentation found no license declaration. This is an open legal gap that must be escalated (e.g., contacting the dataset's maintainers) before any public release of a model or derivative dataset trained on it — not merely a documentation inconvenience.

3. **Cross-language comparison (RQ3) is confounded by dataset origin.** The Arabic and English primary pools differ simultaneously in label-generation process, sub-domain, collection era/region, and scale. Any observed Arabic-vs-English performance gap could be explained by these confounds rather than a true language-difficulty effect. Mitigation (a small matched-protocol validation slice) is planned but not yet executed — until it exists, all cross-language comparisons on the pooled data must be reported as exploratory, not causal.

4. **No external, accessible Arabic-English code-switched sentiment dataset could be obtained.** EESA and SESA-CSD exist as published benchmarks but have no confirmed public download path. Code-switching is therefore evaluated via a self-collected analysis lens (CMI/script-ID stratification) rather than an established benchmark — the volume of genuinely code-switched material in the final corpus is currently unknown and may turn out to be small.

5. **Rating-derived labels are not a substitute for human sentiment annotation.** Where used (the English primary pool, and partially the Jordanian-Arabic set via LLM labeling), rating/text disagreement (sarcasm, apologetic low ratings, mixed-sentiment reviews) is a known, real phenomenon that will introduce label noise. This is mitigated by validating the conversion rule against a small hand-labeled gold sample, but not eliminated.

6. **Several load-bearing datasets are very recent (2025–2026) research releases** (both primary Zenodo Arabic datasets, the SESA-CSD and SwitchEmbed papers). Recency means less time for community scrutiny, replication, or error correction than older, more established resources — this should be stated as a mild provenance caveat, not overstated as a flaw.

7. **Yelp-family and TripAdvisor datasets, if used at all, carry non-commercial/no-redistribution restrictions** inherited from the original source regardless of which mirror or re-host is used. They are reserved for external/robustness evaluation only and must never be described as freely licensed.

8. **The "Google Maps" framing in the project title is currently accurate for the chosen primary sources but is conditional.** Both current primary picks (Zenodo 17011574, Zenodo 22061934 for Arabic; UCSD/McAuley for English) are genuinely Google-Maps-sourced. If Phase 2 implementation substitutes a non-Google-Maps dataset (e.g., Yelp) as a primary training source rather than a reserved set, the title and all related claims must be revised — this is not yet at risk, but must be re-checked, not assumed, at each stage. **Separately, none of the primary data was collected by this project** — it is entirely third-party releases (two independent Zenodo deposits plus one lab's bulk scrape). This does not make the "Google Maps reviews" label inaccurate (the reviews are genuinely sourced from that platform regardless of who collected them), but the manuscript's data section should state plainly that the corpus is assembled from existing public releases, not freshly collected by the authors, to pre-empt an obvious reviewer question.

9. **Novelty claim is narrow and must stay narrow.** The defensible gap is the specific intersection of code-switching × review domain × document-level 3-class sentiment — not code-switched Arabic-English sentiment in general (which has 2024–2026 precedent: EESA, SwitchEmbed, SESA-CSD) and not Arabic Google Maps review sentiment in general (which has 2023–2025 precedent: AraMAMS, the tourism ABSA paper). Overclaiming beyond this narrow intersection would not survive peer review.

10. **No guarantee of publication or Q1 acceptance.** This document records what the evidence supports methodologically; it says nothing about venue fit, reviewer taste, or competitive timing, none of which can be assessed in Phase 1.

11. **The literature search underpinning the novelty claim has an undisclosed language/venue blind spot.** The search channels used (ACL Anthology, arXiv, Springer, MDPI, ScienceDirect, SciTePress, ResearchGate, Google Scholar — see `research_raw/literature_review_raw.md` header) are all English-indexed. Arabic-language theses, Gulf-region university repositories, or regional Arabic conference proceedings could in principle contain prior work on the exact gap claimed (code-switching × review domain × document-level 3-class sentiment) without surfacing in these channels. The novelty claim in `research_gap.md` §3 is therefore provisional on this blind spot as well as on Phase 2 execution — a supplementary search pass over Arabic-language venues is recommended before the claim is finalized for a manuscript.

## Phase 2 data-processing addendum (confirmed by direct computation, 2026-10-24)

12. **The Jordan restaurant dataset (Zenodo 22061934) has been directly inspected and confirmed to contain zero Neutral-labeled reviews** — it is strictly binary (Positive/Negative) at both the LLM-labeled (8,364 rows) and human-gold (300 rows) level. This was previously recorded as "unknown" class balance in the Phase 1 audit; it is now a confirmed fact, not a gap. This dataset cannot contribute any Neutral training examples to the 3-class task.

13. **The combined-corpus sentiment/label-source percentages computed in `results/tables/phase2_corpus_stats.md` are almost entirely driven by the English subsample, not a balanced Arabic/English view.** Of ~1,050,832 usable documents across the three primary sources, 1,038,643 (98.8%) are the English (Wyoming+Vermont) sample and only 12,189 (1.2%) are Arabic. Any aggregate percentage (e.g. "80.7% Positive") reflects the English rating-derived distribution almost exclusively; it is not evidence about Arabic sentiment distribution and must not be reported as a balanced cross-language figure without stratifying by language, exactly as `dataset_audit.md` §7 anticipated as a confound.

14. **The CMI-style script-mixing heuristic (methodology_plan.md §4-5) over-counts some non-code-switched cases.** Manual inspection of the 109 documents it flagged as "mixed_arabic_english" found genuine intra-sentence code-switching in some (e.g. embedded English words inside an Arabic sentence) but also several cases that are a full Arabic paragraph followed by its own full English translation — a bilingual-pair structure, not organic code-switching. The heuristic needs refinement (e.g. requiring script alternation within a sliding window, not just overall character-fraction mixing) before its count is used as a precise Group D definition in any reported result.

15. **The English sample (Wyoming + Vermont) was selected as a practical fallback, not a region-matched comparison set.** The UCSD/McAuley 2021 release is confirmed US-only (no Middle East/Gulf coverage exists in this version), so true geographic comparability with the Saudi/Jordanian Arabic data was not achievable; this reinforces limitation #3's dataset-origin confound rather than resolving it.

## Phase 3 addendum (confirmed during model training, 2026-10-24)

16. **All transformer results (MARBERT, XLM-R, across E1/E2/E4/E5) are LoRA-adapted, not fully fine-tuned.** Full fine-tuning was attempted first and OOM'd during the Adam optimizer step on this project's CPU-only, 8.3GB-RAM machine (`docs/decisions.md` D14). LoRA adapts only ~0.3% of parameters via low-rank matrices — a real, citable technique, but not equivalent to standard fine-tuning, and may understate what these architectures could achieve with adequate compute. Every table/figure reporting these results carries an explicit `LoRA_adapted_NOT_full_finetune` flag (`results/tables/model_comparison.md`); this must never be dropped when the numbers are cited in the manuscript.

17. **The classical-baseline-beats-transformer pattern observed on E1 and E2 (and, more weakly, E5) does not survive multiple-comparison correction.** Raw pairwise p-values for several baseline-vs-transformer macro-F1 gaps were below 0.05 (e.g. LogReg vs. XLM-R on E1, p=0.015), but after Holm-Bonferroni correction across all comparisons in the same experiment, none remain significant at the conventional threshold (adjusted p≥0.09 throughout; `results/tables/significance_tests.json`). The honest conclusion is "classical baselines scored numerically higher under this phase's LoRA/CPU constraints, but the difference is not statistically significant at these sample sizes" — not "classical ML beats transformers for this task."

18. **E4 (combined multilingual training) was not completed** — 3 consecutive OOM failures across decreasing batch sizes (`docs/decisions.md` D15) on the same memory-constrained machine. The research question it targets (does multilingual training help or hurt each language) remains open pending access to a machine with more available memory.

19. **E2's test labels are rating-derived, not human-verified**, a limitation already flagged at the dataset level (`label_provenance_policy.md`) but worth restating here at the results level: any E2/E3/E5(English-test) accuracy or macro-F1 figure is partly a measure of agreement with a star-rating-derived proxy label, not confirmed human sentiment ground truth. The Phase 3 error analysis (`error_analysis.md`) found that roughly 50-60% of E2's model errors trace back to this rating/text disagreement mechanism rather than genuine model weakness — this is likely depressing E2's measured macro-F1 ceiling for every model family, not just the weaker ones.

20. **The classical-vs-transformer comparison is confounded by an asymmetric class-imbalance handling choice, not just by LoRA capacity.** The classical baselines (`scripts/run_baselines.py`) explicitly grid-searched `class_weight ∈ {None, "balanced"}` on validation, and `"balanced"` won every single comparison. The transformer training loop (`scripts/run_transformers.py`) uses the Hugging Face default unweighted cross-entropy loss — class weighting was never tested for the transformers, due to time constraints, not a deliberate ablation choice. `results/tables/per_class_results.csv` shows this asymmetry concretely: every LoRA transformer has sharply lower Neutral-class recall (0.11-0.18) than every classical baseline (0.36-0.61), i.e. the transformers default to predicting Positive/Negative far more often and essentially stop predicting Neutral. **Some, possibly most, of the transformers' lower aggregate macro-F1 may be attributable to this untested class-weighting gap rather than to LoRA capacity or CPU constraints** — this must be stated as an open confound in the manuscript, not resolved by asserting either cause.

## Phase 3B addendum (Kaggle GPU full fine-tuning, integrated 2026-10-26)

21. **Phase 3A (laptop/LoRA) and Phase 3B (Kaggle GPU/full fine-tuning) differ in multiple factors simultaneously — any score difference between them is NOT attributable to hardware alone.** Fine-tuning method (LoRA vs. full), class-weighting (unweighted vs. weighted loss), and max_seq_length (64 vs. 128) all changed at once alongside the hardware. `configs/training.yaml`'s `phase3b_gpu.non_causal_framing_requirement` and `docs/decisions.md` D17 state this explicitly: never write or imply "the GPU caused the improvement" — write "the full-fine-tuning/weighted-loss/GPU configuration scored higher than the LoRA/unweighted/CPU configuration," naming the bundle of changes, not a single cause.

22. **Two Phase 3B primary results (standalone MARBERT and standalone XLM-R on Arabic) lost their original row-level predictions.** Paired bootstrap significance testing for these two models substitutes reproducibility-rerun predictions (MARBERT rerun macro-F1=0.765263 vs. primary 0.785025; XLM-R rerun 0.740126 vs. primary 0.740295) — close to, but not identical to, the primary figures. Any statistical claim involving standalone MARBERT or standalone XLM-R-Arabic is therefore technically a claim about the rerun, not the headline primary number, even though both are reported side by side. This must be stated explicitly wherever those significance tests are cited (`results/phase3b_kaggle_gpu/README_PHASE3B.txt` already does this).

23. **After Holm-Bonferroni correction, most Phase 3B pairwise comparisons among the strong models are NOT statistically significant** — e.g. XLM-R-standalone-rerun vs. MARBERT-rerun (adjusted p=0.355), XLM-R-standalone-rerun vs. XLM-R-Multilingual (adjusted p=0.101), English XLM-R-standalone vs. Multilingual (adjusted p=0.508). The comparisons that DO survive correction are specifically each strong in-domain model beating its own zero-shot cross-lingual transfer counterpart (e.g. MARBERT-rerun vs. EN→AR zero-shot, adjusted p=0.006; Multilingual vs. AR→EN zero-shot, adjusted p=0.007) — the data supports "cross-lingual zero-shot transfer underperforms in-language/multilingual training," not "model X beats model Y" among the strong models themselves.

24. **The deployment model (`backend/model/`) is a separate training pass from the Phase 3B primary research E4 multilingual result and must never be cited interchangeably.** Deployment validation macro-F1 (0.7999539, aggregate across both languages during training) is not directly comparable to the research E4 per-language test figures (Arabic=0.767865, English=0.739722) — different metric (validation, not test), different aggregation (combined, not per-language), and possibly a different checkpoint/run. Ad-hoc manual testing of the live deployment API (see `docs/decisions.md` D17) found visibly weaker and lower-confidence predictions on hand-typed Arabic examples than English ones — consistent with this project's broader Arabic/Neutral-class weakness findings, not a backend defect, but a real reliability caveat for anyone using the deployed API, especially for Arabic input. This ad-hoc testing is an **end-to-end functional sanity check only** — it must never be cited as accuracy, validation-accuracy, test-accuracy, or effectiveness evidence (see `README.md`'s backend section and `paper/notes/final_publication_readiness_audit.md` §12).

## Human-Gold / Phase 4 addendum (human-adjudicated reference set, confirmed 2026-10-27)

25. **The human-adjudicated reference set ("Human-Gold," `paper/annotation/full/human_gold_757.csv`) was constructed by a single adjudicator for all 179 disagreement cases (23.6% of the full 757-review set), with no second adjudicator and no adjudicator-vs-adjudicator reliability check.** This is the largest known methodological weakness of the reference set — if that one adjudicator carries any idiosyncratic bias, it propagates uncontested into nearly a quarter of the data used for the Human-Gold re-evaluation phase. A second-adjudicator disagreement-subsample study is identified as a HIGH VALUE (not required) strengthening analysis (`paper/notes/final_publication_readiness_audit.md` §13) and has deliberately not been run as part of the pre-manuscript fixes phase.
26. **"Neutral is the hardest class" must never be stated bare in publication-facing text.** Neutral obtained the lowest per-class F1 of the three classes for every one of 16 evaluated models, under both the original labels (mean F1=0.396) and the human-adjudicated reference set (mean F1=0.176) — but Human-Gold Neutral support is only 42/757 overall (Arabic 9/369, English 33/388), and Neutral was also the class most frequently revised during independent adjudication (82.9% of originally-Neutral-labeled reviews). The exact recommended wording is in `paper/notes/FINAL_SCIENTIFIC_FRAMING.md` ("Claims requiring qualification") and must be used verbatim or near-verbatim, not paraphrased back down to the bare claim.
27. **The UCSD/McAuley English dataset's unresolved license (item #2 above) was re-verified live against the official source page on 2026-10-27 and confirmed still unresolved** — no license, terms of use, or redistribution statement exists on the official page or the general datasets index page; only a citation requirement is stated. The escalation (contacting the McAuley Lab) recommended in Phase 1 (`docs/decisions.md` D2) has still not been performed. See `paper/notes/DATA_LICENSING_AND_RELEASE.md` for the full conservative release plan adopted in the interim.
28. **Several reproducibility items for the Phase 3B Kaggle training environment could not be recovered from any preserved artifact**: the exact Python/PyTorch/CUDA versions used during Kaggle training, the specific CPU model used for Phase 3A, and the Kaggle-side implementation script that produced the original Phase 3B significance-testing outputs (`arabic_paired_bootstrap_holm_results.csv`, `english_paired_bootstrap_holm_results.csv` — the CSVs are preserved, the code that generated them is not). See `paper/notes/REPRODUCIBILITY_MANIFEST.md` for the complete recovered/unrecoverable inventory. None of these gaps were filled by guessing.
