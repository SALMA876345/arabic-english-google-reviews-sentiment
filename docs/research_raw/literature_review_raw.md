## 1. Arabic Sentiment Analysis (incl. dialect variation: MSA vs. Gulf/Levantine/Egyptian/Maghrebi)

- **Abu Farha, I. & Magdy, W. et al.** — foundational Arabic SA line; see survey entries below for consolidated citations (older seminal lexicon/CNN-LSTM work from ~2018, e.g. combined CNN+LSTM model, arXiv:1807.02911 — verified search hit, title "A Combined CNN and LSTM Model for Arabic Sentiment Analysis").
- **Shi, Z. & Agrawal, R. Full-text detail on dialect/code-switching gaps not extractable from the fetched abstract alone; treat as corroborating but not the primary gap-detail source.
- **"A comprehensive survey of arabic sentiment analysis"** (older survey, pre-2021) — ResearchGate: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327523158 — found via search, not independently fetched; treat as background-only, do not rely on specifics without further check.
- **"A Survey on Dialect Arabic Processing and Analysis: Recent Advances and Future Trends."** ACM (2025/2026), DOI via dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3747290. Found via search (title + DOI consistent across two independent search hits — ACM page and PDF link). Not independently fetched for full text; cite cautiously (confirm DOI resolves before paper submission).
- **DialectSentEval 2026: Arabic Dialect Sentiment Analysis and Swapping Shared Task.** Organizers: Saad Ezzini, Shadi Abudalfa, Maram Alharbi, Salmane Chafik, Hind Saleh Alatawi, Mo El-Haj, Ahmed Abdelali, Osamah Alnahari, Salima Lamsiyah. Hosted at ArabicNLP 2026 (co-located shared task). ACL Anthology overview: https://aclanthology.org/2026.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.172/ ; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06298. VERIFIED (consistent across multiple independent ACL Anthology system-paper pages, e.g. .174, .183, .184, .185, .186 — all real, indexed shared-task submissions). Subtask 1 = 3-class, multi-dialect sentiment classification across five dialects: **Moroccan, Egyptian, Lebanese, Jordanian, Saudi**. Subtask 2 = sentiment-polarity-swapping (generative). This is squarely the "already well covered" bucket for dialect-variation sentiment classification — very recent, active shared task with ~8+ participant system papers.
- **AHaSIS: Shared Task on Sentiment Analysis for Arabic Dialects.** Organizers: Maram I. Alharbi, Salmane Chafik, Saad Ezzini, Ruslan Mitkov, Tharindu Ranasinghe, Hansi Hettiarachchi. - **MARSA: Multi-Domain Arabic Resources for Sentiment Analysis.** ResearchGate: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355348624. Found via search: largest sentiment-annotated corpus for Gulf dialectal Arabic, 61,353 manually labeled tweets. Not independently fetched — treat dataset-size claim as reported-by-search-summary, confirm before citing exact numbers.
- Multiple additional 2023-2024 Saudi-dialect sentiment papers exist (e.g. "Sentiment Analysis of Saudi Dialect Using Deep Learning Techniques," IEEE, https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8706408; "Sentiment lexicon for sentiment analysis of Saudi dialect tweets," ScienceDirect) — both are real search hits, Twitter/lexicon-based, pre-transformer-era for the lexicon one.

**Assessment:** Arabic sentiment analysis with dialect variation is a mature, heavily worked sub-field, with active 2025-2026 shared tasks (AHaSIS, DialectSentEval) explicitly targeting multi-dialect 3-class classification. Gulf/Saudi and Levantine/Egyptian/Moroccan dialects are all represented in recent shared-task data. **Do not claim novelty for "Arabic dialect sentiment classification" per se — this is saturated.**

VERIFIED search hit, real and well-known paper (XLM-T twitter-xlm-roberta models); covers multiple languages, Twitter-domain, not Arabic-specific but commonly used as a cross-lingual SA baseline.
- Cross-lingual SA survey results (search-summarized, not independently fetched): XLM-R generally outperforms mBERT on Arabic SA tasks (e.g., one reported comparison: XLM-R F1 0.60 vs. mBERT-F1 0.48 on an unspecified Arabic test set) — **this specific number is from an AI search summary, not a verified paper**; flag as indicative only, re-derive empirically in our own baselines rather than citing.
- "Cross-Lingual Sentiment Analysis of E-Commerce Product Review" — ResearchGate, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386985649 — found via search; e-commerce domain, cross-lingual, not Arabic-specific per snippet (unclear language pair) — needs fuller read before citing.

**Assessment:** Cross-lingual/multilingual SA involving Arabic as one of several languages in a multilingual benchmark is reasonably well covered (XLM-R/mBERT comparisons exist). What is **not** common is cross-lingual work that treats Arabic-English as a *single mixed input stream* (i.e., genuine code-switching) rather than as two separate monolingual corpora evaluated under one multilingual model — that distinction matters for our gap (see §4).

## 4. Arabic-English Code-Switching in NLP, and Code-Switched SENTIMENT Analysis Specifically

This is the most safety-critical section — verified with direct page fetches, not just search summaries.

- **Hamed, I., Sabty, C., Abdennadher, S., Vu, N. T., Solorio, T., Habash, N. (2025).** "A Survey of Code-switched Arabic NLP: Progress, Challenges, and Future Directions." *Proceedings of the 31st International Conference on Computational Linguistics (COLING 2025).* ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.307/ ; arXiv:2501.13419. **VERIFIED by direct fetch** — confirmed title, full author list, venue, year, abstract. Directly fetched abstract text did **not** surface sentiment analysis, Arabic-English-specific datasets, or Google Maps/review-domain content (abstract-level only; full paper may discuss SA as one downstream task in its body — abstract alone is inconclusive on this point, so do not claim the survey omits it entirely, only that the abstract doesn't highlight it).
- **Sherif, A. & Sabty, C. (2024).** "Sentiment analysis for Egyptian Arabic-English code-switched data using traditional neural models and advanced language models." In *Speech and Computer: 26th International Conference, SPECOM 2024, Belgrade, Serbia, November 25–28, 2024, Proceedings, Part II*, pp. 54–69, Springer (Berlin, Heidelberg). Springer book DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-78014-1. **VERIFIED** — consistent details (authors, exact page range, venue, dataset size, methods) appear identically across independent search results referencing the Springer proceedings listing and citing papers (SwitchEmbed, below). Dataset: **4,100 Egyptian-English YouTube comments**, 3-class sentiment. Methods: BiLSTM+self-attention, hybrid transformer with Arabic/Arabic-English embeddings, ensemble. This paper is also the origin of the **"EESA" benchmark** (2,464 train / 818 dev / 818 test instances, 3-class) referenced by later work (SwitchEmbed, below) as "EESA (Sherif and Sabty, 2024)."
- **Rizkallah, M., Ghonim, A., Sherif, A., Sabty, C. (2026).** "SwitchEmbed: Representation Learning for Arabic-English Code-Switched Text." *Proceedings of the 15th International Conference on Data Science, Technology and Applications (DATA 2026)*, Vol. 1, pp. 712–719. German International University, Egypt. SciTePress: https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2026/151951/151951.pdf. **VERIFIED by direct fetch** (full author list, affiliation, venue, page numbers, abstract, method names SwitchMLM/ConCSE, and numeric results all extracted directly from the source). Introduces **CS-SNLI** (86,141-triplet Arabic-English code-switched NLI dataset, Egyptian Arabic-English, extension of SNLI) and evaluates representations on the EESA sentiment benchmark and an Arabic-English NER dataset. Sentiment F1 results: baseline mBERT 79.24 / XLM-R 81.78; SwitchMLM improves to 81.41 (mBERT) / 84.05 (XLM-R); ConCSE to 79.70 / 83.77. This is a genuine, real, recent (2026) **Arabic-English code-switched sentiment classification** paper — directly contradicts any claim that no one has done CS Arabic-English sentiment before.
- **Alghamdi, S., Rayson, P., Alotibi, R. (2026).** "Improving on State-of-the-Art Models for Sentiment Analysis on Saudi-English Code-Switching Text." *2nd Workshop on NLP for Languages Using Arabic Script (AbjadNLP 2026)*, Rabat, Morocco. ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2026.abjadnlp-1.30/ (also listed on Lancaster University research directory). **VERIFIED by direct fetch** (title, authors, venue, abstract, dataset name, models, and numeric results all confirmed from the ACL Anthology page itself). Introduces **SESA-CSD** (Saudi-English Sentiment Analysis Code-Switching Dataset) — described as the first Saudi-English code-switching dataset for sentiment analysis, 3-class (positive/negative/neutral). Tested models: XLM-RoBERTa, GigaBERT, SaudiBERT, Fanar, ALLaM. Result: 3-11 point improvement over baseline; **XLM-RoBERTa best among "small" models at 95.50% accuracy / 95.53% macro-F1**; multilingual/Arabic small models outperformed bilingual Arabic-English LLMs across zero-shot/few-shot settings.
- **Prompt-based few-shot comparison paper**, ScienceDirect: "A comparative study of prompt-based few-shot learning for Arabic and code-switched NLP," https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949719126000178 — found via search; reportedly evaluates PET/LM-BFF on Arabic and Arabic-English code-switched classification tasks including sentiment. Not independently fetched for full verification — treat the specific "~1.5 F1-point improvement" and "ArEnSA" dataset-name claims from search summaries as **UNVERIFIED**.
- **"ArEnSA" dataset** — appeared ONLY inside AI-generated search-result summary text, never as an actual titled search hit, paper, or page we could independently fetch. A dedicated search for `"ArEnSA" dataset sentiment Arabic English paper citation` returned no corroborating source. **Flag as UNVERIFIED / possibly a search-summarizer hallucination or confusion with EESA. Do NOT cite "ArEnSA" in the paper.**
- **Parallel-language-pair precedent (not Arabic):** "Sentiment Analysis of Code-Switched Filipino-English Product and Service Reviews Using Transformers-Based Large Language Models," ResearchGate: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378581258. Real, verified search hit — useful as a methodological precedent showing code-switched *review-domain* sentiment has been tackled for another language pair (Filipino-English), reinforcing that the Arabic-English + review-domain combination is the gap, not code-switched sentiment analysis in general.
- Related, NOT sentiment — language identification / code-switching detection papers that must not be conflated with sentiment work: "Identifying Code-switching in Arabizi" (ACL Anthology, WANLP 2022, https://aclanthology.org/2022.wanlp-1.18.pdf); "Language Identification of Intra-Word Code-Switching for Arabic–English" (ScienceDirect); "Creating a Large Multi-Layered Representational Repository of Linguistic Code Switched Arabic Data" (arXiv:1909.13009); "Contextual Embeddings for Arabic-English Code-Switched Data" (ResearchGate, likely Nagoudi et al.). These are LID/embedding papers, not sentiment classification — they establish the broader CS-Arabic-NLP context but are not direct competitors to our task.

**Assessment — this is the single most important finding for novelty framing:**
Genuine Arabic-English code-switched SENTIMENT classification **does exist** and is **very recent** (SPECOM 2024, DATA 2026, AbjadNLP 2026 — i.e., essentially all within the last ~24 months, with two of the three papers dated 2026). It is **not** a crowded field — three papers/benchmarks identified (EESA/Sherif & Sabty 2024; SwitchEmbed/Rizkallah et al. 2026; SESA-CSD/Alghamdi et al. 2026) — but it is **not virgin territory either**. Both existing CS-sentiment efforts are regionally/domain narrow: EESA is Egyptian-English **YouTube comments**; SESA-CSD is Saudi-English (domain unspecified in abstract, likely social-media). **Neither is sourced from Google Maps / location-business reviews.** This is the precise, defensible angle: the *combination* of (a) Arabic-English code-switching, (b) 3-class sentiment, and (c) the Google Maps / location-review domain, has no identified prior work. Claiming "first code-switched Arabic-English sentiment paper" would be **false and must be avoided**; claiming "first in the Google-Maps-review domain" or "first spanning Gulf/Levantine/Egyptian dialects + code-switching + review domain jointly" is defensible pending final dataset-discovery confirmation.

## 5. Google Maps / Location / Business Review Sentiment Analysis (as distinct from Yelp/TripAdvisor)

- **AraMAMS / AraMA: "AraMAMS: Arabic Multi-Aspect, Multi-Sentiment Restaurants Reviews Corpus for Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis."** Published in *Sustainability* (MDPI), DOI: 10.3390/su151612268 (2023). VERIFIED via DOI resolving consistently across multiple independent search hits (MDPI DOI link, ResearchGate mirror, encyclopedia.pub summary). Source data: **10,750 Google Maps reviews for restaurants in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia** (AraMA, first/largest version), plus a filtered AraMAMS subset of 5,312 multi-sentiment reviews. 4 aspect categories (food, environment, service, price) × 4 sentiment polarities (positive, negative, neutral, conflict). Dialectal Saudi Arabic, annotated by Arab annotators able to read Saudi dialect. **This is real, directly-on-domain prior work for Arabic Google Maps reviews — but it is aspect-based (ABSA), not document-level 3-class, is monolingual Arabic (not code-switched/bilingual), and is restaurant-only / Riyadh-only.**
- **"Multi-Aspect Sentiment Classification of Arabic Tourism Reviews Using BERT and Classical Machine Learning."** Published in *Data* (MDPI), DOI: 10.3390/data10110168 (2025). Found via search, consistent DOI across two independent hits. Source: Google Maps reviews of **tourist attractions across Saudi Arabia**, manually annotated, >3,500 reviews, 6 aspects (price, cleanliness, facilities, service, environment, overall experience). Again aspect-based, monolingual Arabic, Saudi-only.
- **Arabic café reviews dataset** (Google Maps, Riyadh), 3,000 reviews, 3 annotators, aspect + 3-class sentiment polarity per aspect — found via search (Zenodo record for a related "Multi-Aspect Sentiment Analysis of Arabic Café Reviews" paper, https://zenodo.org/records/17781916). Not independently fetched for full metadata; treat dataset size as reported, re-confirm before citing precisely.
- **AHaSIS (hotel reviews)** — see §1 — closest *non-Google-Maps* review-domain precedent (hospitality, dialectal Arabic, document-level 3-class).
- **English-language / non-Arabic Google Maps sentiment papers found (confirm the domain is well-studied for English, reinforcing that the Arabic+code-switched angle is the real gap):**
  - "Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis of Google Maps Reviews Using LLMs" (Springer chapter, 2025/2026) — uses Gemini LLMs + Streamlit dashboard, English-language pipeline, no Arabic/code-switching mentioned in search snippet.
  - "Sentiment Analysis of Google Maps User Reviews on the Play Store Using Support Vector Machine and LDA" (ResearchGate).
  - "Utilizing Google Map Reviews and Sentiment Analysis: Knowing Customer Experience in Coffee Shops" (ResearchGate).
  - "Exploring the reviews of Google Maps to assess the user opinions about public libraries" (ResearchGate).
  - "Crowdsourcing public attitudes toward local services through the lens of Google Maps reviews: An urban density-based perspective" — arXiv:2404.13156, Atlanta case study, English, urban-planning focus, not sentiment-classification-method focused.
  - "Analysis on Review Data of Restaurants in Google Maps through Text Mining: Focusing on Sentiment Analysis" (Semantic Scholar) — appears Korean/English restaurant text-mining study.
  - None of these English-language Google Maps papers involve Arabic or code-switching per the retrieved snippets.

**Assessment:** Google Maps review sentiment is a real, active sub-domain, but with a clear split: (a) English-language Google Maps sentiment work exists and is mostly applied/industry-style (SVM, LDA, LLM pipelines), not benchmark-building; (b) Arabic Google Maps review work exists but is **aspect-based, monolingual dialectal Arabic, Saudi/Riyadh-centric, and restaurant/tourism-domain**. **No identified paper performs document-level 3-class sentiment classification on Google Maps reviews that are Arabic-English bilingual/code-switched.** This is consistent with and reinforces the code-switching gap identified in §4 — the two gaps (domain + language-mixing) compound rather than overlap with existing work.

## 6. Transformer-Based Sentiment Baselines: AraBERT, MARBERT, ARBERT, CAMeLBERT, XLM-R, mBERT

- **Antoun, W., Baly, F., Hajj, H. (2020).** "AraBERT: Transformer-based Model for Arabic Language Understanding." *Proceedings of the 4th Workshop on Open-Source Arabic Corpora and Processing Tools, with a Shared Task on Offensive Language Detection* (OSACT4, LREC 2020), Marseille, France. ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2020.osact-1.2/ ; arXiv:2003.00104. **VERIFIED** — authors (American University of Beirut), venue, and year all confirmed consistently. Code/models: github.com/aub-mind/arabert.
- **Abdul-Mageed, M., Elmadany, A., Nagoudi, E. M. B. (2021).** "ARBERT & MARBERT: Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Arabic." *Proceedings of ACL-IJCNLP 2021* (ACL 2021 long paper). ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.551/ ; arXiv:2101.01785. **VERIFIED** — consistent across multiple independent search hits (ACL Anthology PDF, arXiv). ARBERT = MSA-focused; MARBERT = trained on a much larger/more diverse (incl. dialectal, social-media) corpus; MARBERT generally reported to outperform ARBERT on dialectal sentiment tasks because of pretraining-domain match.
- **Inoue, G., Alhafni, B., Baimukan, N., Bouamor, H., Habash, N. (2021).** "The Interplay of Variant, Size, and Task Type in Arabic Pre-trained Language Models" (CAMeLBERT). *Proceedings of WANLP 2021 (EACL 2021 workshop)*. ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2021.wanlp-1.10.pdf. **VERIFIED** — authors, venue (EACL 2021, WANLP), and GitHub repo (CAMeL-Lab/CAMeLBERT) all consistent across independent hits. Four model variants (MSA, dialectal, classical Arabic, and Mix), BERT-base, 167GB pretraining corpus. Evaluated on 5 tasks incl. **sentiment analysis**, NER, POS, dialect ID, poetry classification, across 12 datasets. Key finding: variant-proximity of pretraining data to the fine-tuning domain matters more than raw pretraining-data size — directly relevant methodological grounding for why we should consider dialect/domain-matched pretraining when choosing a baseline.
- **Conneau, A. et al. (2020).** "Unsupervised Cross-lingual Representation Learning at Scale" (XLM-R). ACL 2020. Seminal/standard citation for multilingual transformer baseline (not re-verified this session beyond general knowledge; extremely well-established paper, low risk).
- **Devlin, J. et al. (2019).** "BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding." NAACL 2019. Seminal grounding citation; multilingual BERT (mBERT) is a released checkpoint, cite via the official release documentation alongside this paper rather than as a separate authored paper.
- Recent applied benchmarking: "Northwestern at DialectSentEval 2026: A Comparative Study of MARBERTv2 and AraBERT for Arabic Dialect Sentiment Analysis," ACL Anthology https://aclanthology.org/2026.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.177/ — VERIFIED as a real, indexed shared-task system paper; finds MARBERTv2 consistently outperforms AraBERT on 3-class, 5-dialect sentiment under matched settings — directly useful as a baseline-selection justification for our project (favor MARBERTv2 over AraBERT if choosing one Arabic-only backbone).
- SESA-CSD paper (§4) found **XLM-RoBERTa outperforms bilingual Arabic-English LLMs and other Arabic-specific small models (GigaBERT, SaudiBERT) on Saudi-English code-switched sentiment** — an important, directly relevant baseline signal for our own model-selection: a general multilingual encoder may beat Arabic-specific or "bilingual" models precisely because code-switched text doesn't match either monolingual model's pretraining distribution well.

**Assessment:** Baseline model literature is mature and well-documented (AraBERT, ARBERT/MARBERT, CAMeLBERT, XLM-R, mBERT all with verifiable original papers). No gap here — this section is purely for grounding/justifying baseline choices in our methodology, not a novelty claim.

## What's Already Done Extensively (do NOT claim as novel)

1. Arabic sentiment analysis across MSA and major dialects (Gulf/Saudi, Levantine, Egyptian, Moroccan/Maghrebi) — multiple active 2025-2026 shared tasks (AHaSIS, DialectSentEval) with multi-team participation.
2. English review-domain sentiment analysis (Amazon, Yelp, e-commerce) using BERT-family transformers — saturated.
3. Arabic transformer baselines (AraBERT, ARBERT, MARBERT, CAMeLBERT) and their comparative benchmarking on Arabic SA tasks, including dialectal and multi-dialect settings.
4. Multilingual/cross-lingual SA frameworks (XLM-R, mBERT, XLM-T) applied to Arabic as one of several languages in a multilingual benchmark.
5. Arabic Google Maps review mining — **aspect-based**, monolingual-dialectal-Arabic, Saudi/Riyadh-centric (restaurants, cafés, tourist sites). This specific niche is NOT open for a "first Arabic Google Maps sentiment paper" framing.
6. Arabic-English code-switched sentiment classification **exists** (EESA/Sherif & Sabty 2024 on YouTube comments; SwitchEmbed 2026; SESA-CSD/AbjadNLP 2026 on Saudi-English). This is NOT virgin territory — do not claim to be first.
7. Code-switching detection/language-identification for Arabic-English (separate from sentiment) is a long-established sub-field (WANLP shared tasks since 2019+).

## Candidate Defensible Gaps (ranked)

1. **Strongest candidate:** No identified work performs sentiment classification on Arabic-English **code-switched Google Maps / location-business reviews** specifically. Existing CS-Arabic-English sentiment work is YouTube-comment (EESA) or unspecified-domain Saudi social text (SESA-CSD); existing Arabic Google Maps review work is monolingual-dialectal and aspect-based (AraMAMS, tourism ABSA). The intersection — review-domain + code-switching + document-level 3-class — has no confirmed prior paper.
2. **Moderate candidate:** A unified 3-class (not aspect-based) dataset/benchmark spanning pure Arabic (multi-dialect), pure English, AND organically code-switched Arabic-English Google Maps reviews in a single consistent schema and annotation protocol does not appear to exist; most related work isolates one of these three language conditions.
3. **Weaker/needs more support:** Geographic/demographic framing (e.g., UAE-specific Gulf Arabic-English code-switching, given the research context) — no UAE-specific Google Maps Arabic-English CS sentiment paper was found, but this is a narrower geographic slice of gap #1 rather than an independently distinct gap; frame as a specific instantiation, not a separate contribution.

- The "ArEnSA" dataset name surfaced only in AI-generated search summaries with no independently fetchable source — could not confirm whether this is a real but obscure dataset, a mis-transcription of "EESA," or a hallucination. **Do not cite without independent confirmation.**
- Could not fully read the body (only abstracts/summaries) of: Hamed et al. 2025 COLING survey, Shi & Agrawal 2025 NAACL-Findings survey, and the ScienceDirect prompt-based few-shot CS paper — their full texts may contain additional relevant citations or caveats not captured here (PDF binary content could not be parsed by the fetch tool).
