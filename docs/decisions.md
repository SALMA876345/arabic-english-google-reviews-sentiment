### D1 — Arabic dataset: combine Zenodo 17011574 + Zenodo 22061934 as the primary Arabic pool
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** HARD alone (largest, but binary-only, no neutral class, unresolved license); LABR/GLARE (large but off-domain, no/weak sentiment labels); ArSAS (high label quality but Twitter, not reviews); using only Zenodo 17011574 alone (highest quality but only 3,543 reviews — likely too small alone for transformer fine-tuning).
**Evidence:** 12-dimension scoring in `research_raw/quality_audit_raw.md` §2 — Zenodo 17011574 scored 3.6 (highest in the entire pool, Arabic or English), Zenodo 22061934 scored 3.2. Both are Google-Maps-sourced, CC-BY-4.0 (license independently confirmed via Zenodo API for 17011574).
**Rationale:** 17011574 is the only dataset with genuine human annotation (κ = 0.87) and exact domain/platform match; 22061934 adds needed volume. Neither alone is sufficient (one too small, one weak-labeled); combined they cover both label-quality and scale needs, provided the weak-label caveat is documented everywhere.
**Risks:** 22061934's majority-LLM-generated labels; its per-class counts are unpublished (unknown class balance); using 17011574's aspect-level "overall experience" field as a document-level label is a methodological choice, not a given.

### D2 — English dataset: UCSD/McAuley Google Local Review Dataset as primary, pending license resolution
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** Yelp Open Dataset family (larger, more labeled precedent, but academic/non-commercial restriction inherited by every re-host — confirmed not lifted by any mirror); TripAdvisor (business/location-adjacent but CC BY-NC 4.0 and two mirrors disagree on size); Google Play App Reviews (clean 3-class rule but app-store domain, not location reviews).
**Evidence:** Scored 2.9 in `quality_audit_raw.md` §2 — highest English score, driven by domain match (actual Google Maps/Local platform) and scale.
**Rationale:** Only candidate matching the Arabic data's platform (Google Maps), which is required to credibly use "Google Maps" in the project title/claims (see `methodology_plan.md` §3). Its two weaknesses (no native sentiment labels; no confirmed license) are addressable (validated rating-derivation rule; license escalation) rather than disqualifying.
**Risks:** **No confirmed license** — a genuine legal gap, not resolved by this audit; must be escalated (e.g. contacting the McAuley Lab) before any public release of a trained model or derivative dataset. Rating→sentiment conversion must be validated against a hand-labeled gold sample, not assumed reliable, given the known sarcasm/ambivalence failure mode. **Fallback if license escalation fails or goes unanswered** (a realistic outcome — the lab's standard public stance is "cite our papers, no formal license, use at your own judgment"): restrict any model/derivative trained on this data to internal, non-published research use only, and fall back to the SemEval Restaurant ABSA set (smaller, but with a clear academic license) as the English primary pool for any artifact intended for public/camera-ready release.

### D3 — Star-rating-derived labels will be used for the English primary pool, explicitly named as such
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** Fully human-annotating a large English pool from scratch (infeasible within 4 days); using only datasets with native human-annotated sentiment (would eliminate the only Google-Maps-domain English option).
**Evidence:** No English Google-Maps-domain dataset with native human-annotated 3-class sentiment was found by the English scout or the auditor.
**Rationale:** Acceptable provided (a) the term "rating-derived sentiment labels" is used throughout, never "manually annotated ground truth," and (b) the conversion rule is validated against a small hand-labeled gold sample before being trusted.
**Risks:** Rating/text disagreement (sarcastic 5★, apologetic 1★) is a known, real phenomenon, not hypothetical — flagged as an error-analysis category in `methodology_plan.md` §9.

### D4 — No off-the-shelf Arabic-English code-switched sentiment dataset will be used; code-switching is treated as a self-collected analysis lens
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** EESA (Sherif & Sabty 2024) — right label scheme, wrong domain (YouTube), confirmed via two independent search passes to have no public download path; SESA-CSD (Alghamdi et al. 2026) — same issue, no confirmed public release; pursuing direct author contact for either.
**Evidence:** `research_raw/codeswitch_datasets_raw.md` and the auditor's independent re-verification in `research_raw/quality_audit_raw.md` §1.3.
**Rationale:** A 4-day sprint cannot absorb the unpredictable turnaround of an author-data-request. Treating code-switching as a CMI/script-ID-based stratification of the project's own collected reviews (per `methodology_plan.md` §5) turns an external data gap into a methodological contribution rather than a blocker.
**Risks:** The volume of genuinely code-switched (Group D) documents in the primary pools is currently unknown and may be small, since neither primary dataset was built to capture code-switching — this must be measured early in Phase 2 (via the CMI pipeline) before committing to any claim about Group D sample size.

### D5 — No new Google Maps scraping in Phase 1; any Phase 2 fresh collection must use the official Places API or equivalent documented ToS-compliant mechanism
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** uncontrolled scraping (explicitly disallowed by project instruction); relying solely on existing datasets with no fresh collection at all (simpler, but forecloses the matched-protocol validation slice in `methodology_plan.md` §5 unless built by sub-sampling existing pooled data instead).
**Evidence:** Project constraint; no legitimate bulk Google Maps research-licensing mechanism beyond the official Places API was identified during this phase.
**Rationale:** Protects the project from ToS violations and from a title/claims integrity problem (the data must actually be from Google Maps if the title says so).
**Risks:** If Places API access or terms prove infeasible in Phase 2, the fallback (sub-sampling + dual-annotating existing pooled reviews for the matched-protocol slice) must be invoked and stated explicitly, not silently substituted.

### D6 — Models shortlist: TF-IDF+LR, TF-IDF+SVM, MARBERT (primary Arabic-oriented), AraBERT (secondary/ablation), XLM-RoBERTa
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** CAMeLBERT (strong variant-matching rationale, but no specific finding favoring it over MARBERT for this task); adding an LLM zero-shot-prompting arm (deferred — insufficient literature grounding found, see `research_gap.md` §4 open question); building a full "model zoo" (explicitly rejected per project instruction).
**Evidence:** MARBERTv2 > AraBERT on 3-class dialectal sentiment per a verified 2026 DialectSentEval shared-task system paper; XLM-R outperformed bilingual Arabic-English LLMs on Saudi-English code-switched sentiment per the verified SESA-CSD paper.
**Rationale:** Each model answers a specific RQ (RQ2: classical vs. transformer; RQ3: Arabic-specific vs. multilingual on code-switched text) rather than being added for completeness.
**Risks:** None training-related in Phase 1 (no training performed).

### D9 — Phase 2 kickoff: downloaded the three primary datasets and computed real corpus statistics (no placeholders)
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** pulling the full UCSD/McAuley dump (666M reviews, infeasible within sprint compute/time); region-matching the English sample to the Arabic data's Gulf/Levant region (user's first preference) — ruled out after confirming the 2021 UCSD release is US-only with no Middle East/Gulf subset; using only one US state — rejected in favor of two small states (Wyoming + Vermont) for slightly more robust estimates at acceptable download/processing cost (~160MB combined).
**Evidence:** `src/preprocessing/build_corpus_stats.py` (run, not simulated) against the actual downloaded files; output in `results/tables/phase2_corpus_stats.md` and `data/processed/*.csv`.
**Rationale:** The user asked for real computed statistics (raw/usable counts, class distribution, label-source breakdown, mixed-language count, dedup count), which requires actually acquiring and processing data — this is Phase 2 work by definition, so explicit approval was sought (and given) before proceeding, consistent with the Phase 1 stop condition.
**Risks:** Two new structural facts emerged from this processing that were not visible during desk-research-only Phase 1 (see `known_limitations.md` §Phase 2 addendum): the Jordan dataset has zero Neutral examples, and the combined-corpus percentages are overwhelmingly dominated by the English subsample (98.8% of usable documents) — any single aggregate percentage reported from this pass must be read as English-dominated, not a balanced bilingual figure. The script-mixing heuristic used for the "Arabic-English mixed" count is a first-pass heuristic confirmed (by manual spot-check) to include some false positives (parallel bilingual translations, not genuine code-switching) and will need refinement before being used in any reported result.

### D10 — Phase 2.5: the matched Arabic-vs-English comparison target is 3,525 (the Arabic PRIMARY/tourism pool), not 12,189 (the combined Arabic figure including Jordan)
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** matching the English subset to 12,189 as the user's Phase 2.5 instruction literally states (rejected); matching separately to each Arabic sub-pool (rejected as unnecessary complexity given D12's Jordan decision).
**Evidence:** `codeswitch_audit.md`-independent finding that the Jordan dataset (8,664 of the 12,189) is binary-only (zero Neutral) and majority LLM-weak-labeled, confirmed by direct inspection in Phase 2 (`known_limitations.md` #12).
**Rationale:** Matching an English 3-class subset to a combined Arabic figure that is itself ~71% binary/weak-labeled data would silently launder Jordan's incompatible label scheme into "the Arabic side" of a 3-class comparison. The scientifically coherent choice is to match English to the Arabic sub-pool that is actually comparable in kind (3-class, human-annotated): the tourism primary pool, N=3,525.
**Risks:** This is a smaller primary comparison sample than the instruction's literal 12,189 figure implied. Mitigated by Jordan remaining available as training augmentation (D12) and by the full English corpus remaining available for a separate scale experiment (E8) — neither capability is lost, only re-labeled as supplementary rather than primary.

### D11 — Test-set membership for the primary matched comparison is frozen now, by review_id, independent of whether manual annotation is completed
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** waiting until manual annotation (docs/annotation_guidelines.md) is complete before freezing any split (rejected — would block Phase 3 readiness entirely on annotation completion, which the project instruction explicitly allows to remain pending); re-deriving splits later once labels exist (rejected — violates the explicit "test membership must not change" stop condition).
**Evidence:** `src/preprocessing/freeze_splits.py` output; manifests in `data/splits/frozen_v1/`.
**Rationale:** Group-aware splitting (by place for Arabic, by business/gmap_id for English) was applied so no place/business appears in both train and test, then the resulting test-group rows became both the frozen test set AND the manual-annotation candidate set in one step — avoiding the inconsistency that would result from freezing test membership separately from selecting annotation candidates (an earlier draft of this phase made exactly that mistake and was corrected before finalizing, see the superseded `build_validation_slice.py`, since removed).
**Risks:** Group-aware splitting could not hit the target train/val/test fractions or sentiment proportions exactly (Arabic has only 42 place-groups, some very large — e.g. achieved test sentiment was 64.5% Positive vs. the pool's overall 61.4%) — reported as achieved, not forced, in `results/tables/final_dataset_statistics.md`.

### D12 — Jordan dataset (Zenodo 22061934) redirected to training-augmentation + binary-auxiliary-evaluation use; excluded from the primary 3-class matched comparison
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** including Jordan in the primary 3-class comparison by mapping its binary labels onto a 3-class scheme with an invented/predicted Neutral class (explicitly rejected — the project instruction forbids inventing Neutral samples); discarding Jordan entirely (rejected — its 8,364 LLM-weak rows and 300 human-gold rows are still legitimate for the uses described below).
**Evidence:** Direct inspection confirmed zero Neutral examples and majority LLM-generated labels (`known_limitations.md` #12).
**Rationale:** Jordan's data is real and usable, just not for the specific thing (3-class, human-grade comparison) the primary experiment needs. Splitting it by its own natural structure — 300 human-gold reviews reserved as evaluation-only ground truth for a **binary** auxiliary experiment, 8,364 LLM-weak reviews available only as training augmentation (never evaluation) — uses every part of the dataset for something it is actually suited to.
**Risks:** The binary auxiliary experiment is not directly comparable to the primary 3-class results (different task) — must be reported as a separate, clearly-labeled supplementary experiment, never pooled into the main Arabic accuracy figures.

### D13 — English matched subset built with domain restriction (tourism/park/museum/historical-site category proxy) and length-stratified sampling; geography and star-rating distribution explicitly NOT matched
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** matching on geography (rejected — confirmed infeasible, UCSD/McAuley 2021 is US-only with no Middle East/Gulf coverage); matching on star-rating distribution (rejected — the Arabic primary pool has no star ratings at all, so there is nothing to match against); using the full English pool unfiltered by domain (rejected — would compare Arabic tourism-site reviews against English reviews of car dealerships and gas stations, a much larger domain mismatch than necessary given a usable proxy category filter exists).
**Evidence:** `results/tables/arabic_vs_matched_english_comparability.md`; domain-filtered pool size confirmed at 111,527 rows (ample for stratified sampling to N=3,525) in `src/preprocessing/build_matched_english_subset.py`.
**Rationale:** Match what can genuinely be matched (sentiment distribution exactly; review-length distribution approximately — quartile medians align closely, though the English subset retains a longer right tail) and explicitly document what cannot be (geography, rating distribution) rather than silently presenting a partial match as a full one.
**Risks:** The remaining domain gap (Saudi tourist attractions vs. US tourist attractions/parks/museums) and the unmatched long-tail review-length difference are real residual confounds for any cross-language comparison drawn from this subset — carried forward into `known_limitations.md`.

### D14 — Phase 3: full transformer fine-tuning is infeasible on available hardware; switched to LoRA adapters
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Alternatives considered:** reducing batch size/sequence length further while keeping full fine-tuning (tried first — still OOM'd during the Adam optimizer step, since the problem is optimizer-state size for the full ~270-480M parameters, not batch/activation memory); switching to a smaller, non-approved model architecture (rejected — would silently violate the documented methodology's model choice, a worse deviation than keeping the approved architecture and changing the training method); frozen-embedding feature extraction + an external sklearn classifier (considered and bench-tested successfully, but rejected as the primary method because it is a larger conceptual deviation from "fine-tuning" than LoRA is — LoRA still performs genuine gradient-based adaptation of the transformer itself).
**Evidence:** Direct reproduction: a real full-fine-tuning attempt on XLM-R-base crashed with `RuntimeError: ... not enough memory: you tried to allocate 768006144 bytes` during `optimizer.step()`. System check: 8.28GB total RAM, ~1.3GB available at the time (84% already in use by other processes). A LoRA (r=8, query+value target modules) reproduction of the same training step succeeded, with trainable parameters reduced to 887,811 of 278,933,766 (0.32%).
**Rationale:** LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) is a standard, citable fine-tuning method, not an improvised hack — using it keeps the approved model architectures (XLM-R, MARBERT) intact and still performs real gradient-based adaptation, which is a smaller methodological step away from the original plan than either downgrading the model or resorting to frozen features.
**Risks:** LoRA-adapted models may underperform a fully fine-tuned equivalent, especially on this small a training set — every reported transformer result in Phase 3 must state "LoRA-adapted, not full fine-tuning" and this must not be glossed over when comparing against the classical baselines or when discussing whether "transformers outperform classical ML" (a result favoring classical baselines could be partly a LoRA-capacity artifact, not proof that transformers are not useful here — this ambiguity must be stated in the Phase 3 report and `known_limitations.md`, not resolved by assertion).
