# Annotator Instructions — Arabic-English Review Sentiment Study

Thank you for taking part in this study. These instructions are the same for every annotator — please read all of it before starting, even if someone else has described the task to you already.

## What you are doing

You will read 757 short location/business reviews (a mix of Arabic and English) and judge the **sentiment** of each one. You are one of three people doing this independently. Your judgments, together with the other two, will be compared afterward to measure how consistently people agree on sentiment — this is a real research measurement, so your honest, independent first impression matters more than getting a "right answer."

## Work independently — this is essential, not a formality

- **Do not discuss individual reviews with the other annotators while you are annotating.** General questions about the task/instructions are fine; discussing what you think a *specific* review should be labeled is not, until the study tells you otherwise.
- **Do not look at another annotator's answers**, and do not ask them to show you.
- **Do not inspect star ratings, model predictions, or any other label for a review.** You will not be shown any of these in the interface — if you ever find yourself looking for this information elsewhere, stop; it is not part of this task.
- **Do not use ChatGPT, Claude, or any other AI tool to help decide the sentiment of a review.** This study measures human judgment specifically; an AI-assisted answer is not a valid data point here, regardless of whether you think the AI is "right."
- Judge **only the review text** in front of you.
- **Preserve your first independent judgment.** If you're unsure, make your best call rather than seeking outside confirmation.

Discussion between annotators is permitted later, only during the formal adjudication stage, after all three of you have completely finished and your files have been locked in.

## How to judge sentiment

Use the **same rules for Arabic and English reviews** — there is no different standard per language.

Judge the reviewer's **overall net impression** of the place/business/experience, not any single sentence in isolation.

- **Positive** — the overall impression is favorable, even if the reviewer mentions a minor criticism. *("Parking was annoying but the food was amazing, would go again" → Positive.)*
- **Negative** — the overall impression is unfavorable, even if the reviewer mentions something positive. *("Food was fine but the service was so rude we're never going back" → Negative.)*
- **Neutral** — there is no clear positive or negative net lean: the review is purely factual/descriptive, or the positive and negative content genuinely balance out with no net direction.

**If you are uncertain, you must still choose one of Positive, Neutral, or Negative** — do not leave it blank or skip it — and set your confidence to **low**. "I can't tell" is not itself an answer; your best guess under low confidence is.

## Confidence

For every review, also mark how confident you are in your sentiment choice:

- **High** — you're sure.
- **Medium** — reasonably sure, but something about the review gives you pause.
- **Low** — you're genuinely unsure; this was a hard call.

## Mixed sentiment flag — separate from your sentiment label

**Mixed sentiment is not a fourth label.** It is a separate yes/no flag you set in addition to your Positive/Neutral/Negative choice.

Set **mixed = yes** when the review clearly contains *both* positive and negative content — regardless of which way you leaned overall. A review can be:
- Positive overall, but still flagged mixed=yes (e.g., mostly glowing, but with one real complaint).
- Negative overall, but still flagged mixed=yes (e.g., mostly critical, but with one genuine compliment).
- Neutral, often (though not always) because the positive and negative content balanced out — but Neutral reviews that are simply plain/factual with no sentiment at all should be mixed=no.

Set **mixed = no** when the review is one-sided, or has no real sentiment content to begin with.

## Notes

Optional, but please use them whenever you think a note would help someone understand your decision later — especially anything that made the review hard to classify: sarcasm, negation that flips the meaning ("not bad" vs "not good"), an unfamiliar dialect word or phrase, a very short or vague review, an unclear reference, or text that mixes Arabic and English. You do not need to write a note for every review — only when it would genuinely help.

## Using the interface

- Click the review's correct Sentiment button, Confidence button, and Mixed button (in any order). Once all three are set, the interface automatically saves your answer and moves to the next review.
- Type notes any time before or after making your three selections for the current review — they are saved along with the rest.
- Use the **Back** button if you want to revise the review you just answered. It only goes back one step at a time.
- Your progress is saved automatically after every review, in your browser, on your own machine. Closing the browser, refreshing the page, or restarting your computer will not erase your work — reopening the file will resume exactly where you left off.
- When you have completed all 757 reviews (the interface will tell you clearly when you have), click **Export Results** to save your completed file. Send that exported file back exactly as downloaded — please don't rename, edit, or reformat it.

## If something feels unclear

If any review or any part of these instructions feels ambiguous while you're working, make your best independent call, flag it in the notes, and keep going — do not stop to ask another annotator or change your approach partway through. If the instructions themselves seem to need improvement, mention that separately once you're done, so it can be addressed for any future rounds — but please complete this round under the instructions as written, so all three of you are working from the same consistent rules.

Thank you — this is one of the most important pieces of evidence this research project will produce.
